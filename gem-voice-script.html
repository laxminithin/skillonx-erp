Recording notes

  • Lip-sync (panels 9–11): the grandmother's clip only moves her mouth — record her three lines first, then nudge each clip so the mouth opens on the first word. If sync drifts, cut to the girl's reaction and let the line finish over her face.
  • Room for breath: every cue is written a touch short of its shot length on purpose — leave ~0.5s of silence at each cut so lines never collide across the edit.
  • If you use an AI voice: two distinct voices — a mid-warm narrator and an older grandmother. Keep the "…" as commas or ellipses in the input; most engines honour them as pauses. Render each cue as its own file named vo_01.wav … vo_12.wav.
  • Subtitles = free translation: burn these exact lines in as captions. When you dub the series into another language later, you swap only the audio and captions — never a single rendered shot.
  • Series hook: "her story had only begun" is your recurring sign-off. Reuse the phrasing every episode so young viewers learn to expect it.
