### A01  ·  Easy  ·  5 marks  ·  DESCRIPTIVE  ·  CO2
**Question:** Explain mercantilism and why Smith attacked it.

**Expected Key Points:**
- Mercantilism treated gold and surplus as national wealth and favoured export promotion plus import restraint.
- Smith argued wealth is the productivity of labour and that both sides gain from specialisation.
- Protection can enrich a favoured producer while shrinking the nation’s real income.
- Bullion is a veil; consumption and productivity are the ends.
- The critique founds classical liberal trade theory.

### A02  ·  Easy  ·  5 marks  ·  DESCRIPTIVE  ·  CO2
**Question:** State absolute advantage with a two-country numerical sketch.

**Expected Key Points:**
- Absolute advantage exists when a country uses fewer resources per unit than another.
- If Home makes cloth in 5 hours and Foreign in 15, Home has an absolute advantage in cloth.
- Each country then specialises in its absolutely efficient good.
- Smith’s story is the simple division of labour across borders.
- It fails when one country is better at both goods — that is Ricardo’s cue.

### A03  ·  Easy  ·  5 marks  ·  DESCRIPTIVE  ·  CO2
**Question:** Explain comparative advantage and opportunity cost.

**Expected Key Points:**
- Comparative advantage is lower opportunity cost, not necessarily lower absolute cost.
- Opportunity cost is cloth forgone to make wine.
- Even a country that is worse at both goods should specialise in the good it is least bad at.
- Mutual gains arise if the world price lies between autarky price ratios.
- This is the core VTU theorem of trade.

### A04  ·  Easy  ·  5 marks  ·  DESCRIPTIVE  ·  CO1
**Question:** What is the Heckscher-Ohlin theory?

**Expected Key Points:**
- H-O explains trade by relative factor endowments.
- Capital-rich countries export capital-intensive goods; labour-rich countries export labour-intensive goods.
- Technology is assumed similar so that endowments drive comparative advantage.
- It is a supply-side theory of trade patterns.
- Leontief later challenged a naive US test of it.

### A06  ·  Intermediate  ·  10 marks  ·  DESCRIPTIVE  ·  CO1
**Question:** What is the Leontief paradox? Give two later reconciliations taught in IB.

**Expected Key Points:**
- Leontief found US exports less capital-intensive than US imports, against naive H-O.
- Reconciliations include human-capital intensity of US labour, demand reversals, trade barriers, and natural-resource goods.
- The paradox did not kill factor-proportions thinking; it refined measurement.
- Students should not say H-O is ‘false therefore useless’.
- It remains a benchmark model.
